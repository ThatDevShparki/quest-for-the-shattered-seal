# Ari

## The Name

He named it Ari. Short for Aeralon.

It felt right in the way that things feel right when you are exhausted and alone and something has just emerged from your dead father's journal and landed on your knee and looked at you with eyes that carry something you almost recognize. He needed something to call it. He reached for the most present thing he had.

He does not know if it has another name. He does not know if it had a name before. He does not know if it approves of the one he gave it. It has not objected. He is taking that as provisional acceptance.

---

## The Emergence

He was not looking for it. He did not know it was there.

The first sign came in the ceremonial room, during the encounter with the Visage. In the middle of the voice and the chaos, somewhere in the journal the raven stirred. He did not notice it then. He was running.

The night of the arrest, sitting in the cell after the guards had stopped making rounds, the journal moved. Not dramatically. A tremor along the cover, like something breathing inside it. He had been turning the pages for hours by that point, working through his father's notes, and he noticed it the way you notice something at the edge of your attention when everything else has gone quiet.

Then Ari pulled itself out of the pages like smoke finding a shape.

He has thought about this moment many times since. The specific quality of it. Not frightening, which surprised him. Strange and specific and unlike anything he had a framework for, but not frightening. It landed on his knee and ruffled its feathers once and looked at him and something in him that had been braced for the next terrible thing simply did not find one.

He looked at it for a long time. It looked back.

He named it Ari.

---

## The Drawing

After the emergence he went back to the journal looking for where it had come from. He found it eventually, buried in the chaos of the later pages.

What he had taken on first reading to be a two page spread of chaotic overlapping notes, dense scribbles crossed with large aggressive lines, revealed itself on closer examination to be something else. A drawing. Crude and brutalist in execution, the kind of image that gets made when the hand is not fully under the mind's control but the intention is still there underneath. A raven, rendered in the particular style of someone who knows what they are drawing and cannot quite make the instrument cooperate.

This is where Ari lived. His father drew it there.

He does not know when Aeralon made the drawing or what prompted it. He does not know if it was intentional, a deliberate act of care for the familiar during the descent, or something the fracturing mind produced without fully understanding what it was doing. He does not know if the drawing is a cage or a home or something else entirely.

He knows his father drew a raven into the journal in the middle of the madness and something emerged from it in a jail cell in Grainfall when the journal had been carried far enough and the ritual in the ceremonial room had apparently done whatever it needed to do.

He has more questions about this than he has words for right now.

---

## What It Looks Like

A raven. Matte black from beak to tail, with eyes that carry an unsettling quality of attention. It does not watch the way birds watch. It watches the way something old and patient watches, taking inventory, remembering. Its feathers are perfect and unruffled in a way that suggests it has never been caught in weather, never fought for food, never lived the way a real raven lives.

It is always slightly warmer than it should be. He noticed this the first time it landed on his knee and has noticed it every time since. Not hot. Warm in a way that feels deliberate rather than biological, like warmth being offered rather than simply present.

It rides on his left shoulder when it chooses to, which is most of the time. It weighs almost nothing.

---

## How It Behaves

Calm is the word he would use for its baseline state. Almost wise in its stillness, the particular quality of stillness that belongs to things that have been patient for a very long time and have not lost the habit. It sits. It watches. It does not fidget or demand or perform. It is simply present in the way that very settled things are present, taking up exactly the space it occupies and no more.

And then, without warning, it is not calm at all.

The spurts come suddenly and without obvious trigger. Something said or something that shifts in the room and Ari erupts, erratic and chaotic, flying in patterns that have no clear logic, sometimes saying something in the middle of the chaos before the stillness returns. These moments are brief. They resolve back into calm without transition, as though a switch has been thrown in both directions.

He does not know what causes them. He does not know if they are distress or excitement or something else entirely that he does not have a category for. He knows they feel like signal rather than noise, like something trying to break through rather than simply malfunction.

He watches them carefully. He has not yet understood what they are trying to say.

---

## What It Says

It speaks in his father's cadences. This is the first and most disorienting thing about Ari's speech. Not his father's voice exactly, not a mimicry of the specific timbre and register, but the rhythm and the phrasing and the particular way Aeralon assembled sentences when he was thinking aloud about something he found significant.

What comes out during the spurts is not a sentence. It is two fragments, arriving separately, repeating back and forth in the chaos of the erratic flight before the stillness returns.

_never-dark_

_Dawnmere-burns_

Two incomplete phrases. Together they form something that could be a complete thought. Separately they arrive as two different things, as though whatever is trying to be said cannot hold itself together long enough to come out whole. The raven says one and flies and says the other and flies and then lands and is calm again and does not say either until the next spurt.

He has heard this twice now. Both times the same phrases in the same fragmented pattern.

He does not know what to do with them. He cannot tell if this is comfort or warning. Never-dark could mean protection, something watching over, something that does not allow darkness near the Dawnmere name. Dawnmere-burns could mean the bloodline's nature, something that has always been alight, his mother's faith expressed in the only two words a raven can force through its throat.

Or they could mean something else entirely. Never-dark as a description of something relentless. Dawnmere-burns as what happened or what is coming. The warmth he associated with his mother and the light she served, turned around and pointed at him as a warning rather than a comfort.

He is sitting with both interpretations. He does not know which is right. He is listening to a raven repeat two half sentences and he cannot tell if his mother is speaking or something is speaking in her name.

He does not yet know how to listen in the right way. He is paying attention anyway.

---

## Why It Is Cryptic

His father was never mysteriously magical. This is the thing Zeph keeps returning to when he tries to make sense of Ari.

Aeralon was a wizard. He used magic the way he used every other tool, purposefully and in service of the work. His familiar, if he had one, would have been a practical thing. A scout for excavation sites. An extra pair of eyes in dark corridors. Something that served the research and was understood to serve it.

Ari does not feel like that. Ari feels older than that. More cryptic. The warmth that seems deliberate. The eyes that watch in a way that suggests it is not simply observing but considering. The fragments that feel like they are trying to arrive somewhere rather than simply repeating what they have stored.

His father's magic was never like this. His father's magic made sense.

This does not make sense yet. He is paying close attention to the parts of it that do not make sense because those are the parts that tend to be most important.

---

## What He Feels

The first night in the cell, sitting with Ari on his knee after everything that had happened, he thought something that he has not yet found a way to articulate properly.

It was the first living thing that had looked at him since his father died without expecting him to explain himself.

Not the party he was about to meet. Not the guards. Not the person he grabbed on the way out of the ruin. Ari landed on his knee and looked at him and made no demand and asked no question and required nothing from him except to be present, which he was, because he had nowhere else to be.

He is surprised by it. By its existence, by its nature, by the way it confuses him in ways his father's other work never confused him. He is surprised by how much he does not want it to leave.

He named it after his father. It speaks in his father's cadences. It emerged from his father's journal. And yet something about it feels less like his father than like something adjacent to his father, something that was near him for a long time without being his, something that absorbed what it heard without being the source of it.

He does not know what to make of this feeling. He is holding it carefully, the way he holds things he does not yet have the framework to interpret.

---

## What He Does Not Know

Almost everything that matters.

He does not know what Ari actually is. Whether it is a conventional familiar in the technical sense, whether it is something his father created or something his father found or something that found his father, he cannot say. He does not know why it lived in the journal or what the drawing means or whether the journal is still its home in some sense or whether the emergence was permanent.

He does not know how much it understands of what is said around it. It watches with too much attention for him to assume it understands nothing. He does not know how much it remembers of wherever it was before the journal or what those memories contain.

He does not know why it speaks in his father's cadences rather than its own voice, or whether it has a voice of its own that he has not yet heard. He does not know what it is trying to say in the chaotic spurts and whether he will eventually learn to hear it clearly or whether the barrier between its knowing and its saying is permanent.

He does not know its name. Not its real name. Ari is what he gave it because he needed something to call it. Whether that is who it is or simply what it is called for now, he cannot say.

He does not know why his father drew it into the journal. Whether this was protection or containment or love or necessity or some combination of all of those things, he has no answer yet.

He is watching. He is listening. He is carrying it on his left shoulder into whatever comes next.

That is all he knows how to do with it right now. He suspects it is enough to start with.
