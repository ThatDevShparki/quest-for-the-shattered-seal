# The Orryn Society

## What He Remembers

He spent more time in the Orryn Society's halls growing up than he spent at home. This is not an exaggeration. Aeralon's work was centered there and Aeralon had decided his son would come with him wherever he went, which meant the Society became Zeph's primary environment across a significant portion of his childhood. He did his reading there. He ate there often. He fell asleep in its libraries more than once and woke up to find someone had placed a blanket over him without disturbing the book on his chest.

The memories are playful in the way that childhood memories of serious places are playful, filtered through the particular lens of a child who understood almost nothing of what the adults around him were doing and found that enormously freeing. He remembers the study rooms, which had a specific quality of silence that he spent considerable energy trying to violate in ways that would not get him formally expelled. He remembers the libraries, which were enormous and smelled of something old and permanent and deeply satisfying, and which contained enough material to keep a curious child occupied for years without ever reaching the interesting parts. He remembers the people, who were diverse and opinionated and frequently in some form of low-level academic conflict with each other, which he found fascinating in the way that children find adult disagreement fascinating when it is not directed at them.

He remembers large rooms with high ceilings and the particular acoustics of spaces designed to contain important conversations. He remembers occasional formal events where he was expected to be presentable and quiet, which he managed with varying success. He remembers visiting researchers who arrived with unfamiliar accents and unusual areas of expertise and who sometimes crouched down to his level and explained what they were working on, which he always appreciated and usually could not fully follow.

He remembers that his father moved through all of it with a particular authority. Not performed authority, not the kind that announces itself, but the natural authority of someone whose presence is expected and whose opinion is sought. Aeralon did not seem to notice this about himself. Zeph noticed it on his behalf.

## What It Left Behind

The house he grew up in carries the Society inside it. This is something he has always known but has understood more specifically since Aeralon died and he was alone in it with nothing but time and silence and the accumulated weight of decades of scholarly life.

The furniture has a style and a quality that matches the halls he spent his childhood in. The shelves hold artifacts and publications and reference texts that belong to the same world the Society inhabits. The smell of the rooms, particularly the study, is the smell of those libraries, old paper and wood and the faint mineral quality of things that have been near excavated objects for a long time. He grew up in a house that was an extension of an institution, surrounded by its aesthetic and its artifacts and its intellectual atmosphere, without ever having been formally part of it.

There is something strange about this that he has not yet fully articulated. He knows the Society through his father's relationship to it, which means he knows it filtered through love and proximity and the particular distortion of a child's view of an adult world. What he actually knows about the institution itself, its structure and its politics and its relationships and its purposes, is almost nothing. He knows his father was important within it. He knows the rooms. He knows the smell.

That is the extent of it.

## What He Does Not Know

He does not know who leads it or how it is governed or what its relationship is to political power or to other institutions. He does not know whether it operates in one location or across many. He does not know its founding or its history or what it considers its primary purpose to be beyond the general one of archaeological and historical scholarship.

He does not know how it regarded Aeralon in his final years. Whether the Society watched one of its most celebrated members descend into reclusiveness and obsession and said nothing, or tried to intervene and was refused, or simply did not notice, is entirely unknown to him. He does not know if the Society is aware of what Aeralon was working on in Grainfall. He does not know if they have records of his final research. He does not know if they consider that research to belong to them.

He does not know what they think of Aeralon's death. Whether it was mourned formally, acknowledged institutionally, or quietly filed under unfortunate ends for people who pushed too far into things they should not have, he cannot say.

He does not know if they are aware he exists in any meaningful sense. He is Aeralon's son and that carries weight in certain rooms, but he has never been inducted, never corresponded, never established himself as anything other than a child who used to fall asleep in the libraries. Whether the Society considers him connected to them or simply connected to a memory of someone who was, he does not know.

He has had no contact with anyone from the Society since Aeralon's decline. Whether this is because the Society withdrew as Aeralon became less accessible, or because Aeralon actively kept them away from his son the way he kept everything away from his son at the end, or simply because the contact was always through Aeralon and disappeared naturally when Aeralon did, he cannot say.

## What He Feels

Respect was the word he would have used as a child and through most of his adolescence. Not personal respect exactly, not the respect you extend to a person, but the respect you extend to something large and old and serious that your father was clearly part of. The Orryn Society was important. This was self evident. Important people moved through its halls and important work happened in its rooms and important arguments were conducted in its study spaces in careful low voices. Zeph respected it the way a child respects a cathedral, not because he understood it but because the people around him clearly did.

That has shifted as he has gotten older. Not into something negative. Not into resentment or suspicion or distrust. Simply into something farther away, less immediate, less felt. The Society belongs to his father's world and his father's world is the one he is trying to understand from the outside rather than the inside he used to inhabit. The warmth of the childhood memories is still there. The sense that this was a place where interesting things happened and where his father was someone who mattered. But it sits at a remove now, behind glass, viewable but not quite reachable.

He does not know yet what the Society will mean to him when the campaign puts him back in contact with it. He suspects it will be more complicated than either the uncomplicated respect of his childhood or the comfortable distance of his recent years. There are questions only the Society can answer about his father's final research and about what they knew and when and what they chose to do with that knowledge. He has not yet had occasion to ask them.

He is not sure he is ready to hear the answers. He is going to ask anyway.

## The Home He Left Behind

The house is still standing somewhere. Full of furniture that smells like those halls and shelves that hold those artifacts and a study that carries the accumulated atmosphere of decades of work that was connected to an institution he has no formal standing with.

He left it to come to Grainfall. He does not know when he will go back or what he will do with it when he does. It does not feel like his exactly. It feels like a monument to someone else's life that he happened to grow up inside.

But it is the closest thing to a home he has. And every artifact on those shelves and every book on those cases and every piece of furniture carrying that particular wooden scent was put there by a man who brought him along everywhere he went and taught him to love the work.

That is what the Orryn Society means to him at its most essential. Not an institution. Not a professional body. The smell of the house his father made.

He would like to understand what it actually is someday. He suspects the understanding will change something. He is going to pursue it anyway.
