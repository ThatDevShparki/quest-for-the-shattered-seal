# The Ritual

He did not stop.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|His father]] had written of this room in his earlier years, before the madness. Clean and precise descriptions of a ceremonial space matching this one exactly, cross referenced with a ritual he had found in texts so obscure they were considered fiction by most scholars. A ritual of the dead. A calling, designed to reach gods long forgotten by the living world.

His father had been fracturing when he stood here. Losing the thread between thought and page, between the man he was and the thing he was becoming. [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] was not. He was clear headed, making clean connections, reading the room the way his father had taught him to read every room. He understood what he was looking at. And he understood that [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had not finished what this room was built for. Whether from fear, or fracture, or something worse, the ritual had gone unfinished.

He prepared the space the way his father's notes described. Methodically. The braziers first, arranged on the central platform in the configuration [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|the journal]] specified, their positions corresponding to marks worn into the stone from centuries of identical placement. The kindling caught slowly in the still air and the flames rose without wavering, perfectly vertical in a room that had no draft and had not had one in a very long time.

Then the urns. He positioned them according to the diagram, each aligned to a cardinal direction, each bearing inscriptions in a language he could not read but recognized from [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|the journal]]'s annotations as [[Campaign Knowledge/Clues/Sae-th]]. He placed them precisely. His father had been precise before the precision left him, and the notes from the early years were exacting in their detail.

He stood at the center and opened [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|the journal]] to the page where the ritual's words were transcribed. His father's handwriting was steady here, from the period before the deterioration, each character rendered with the careful clarity of a man who understood that what he was recording mattered enough to get right. The words were not in any language [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] recognized. He spoke them anyway, sounding each syllable the way his father had noted, the phonetics annotated in the margins with the thoroughness of a scholar who expected someone else to eventually read them.

His voice was the first sound in that chamber in a very long time. The stone swallowed each word and gave back nothing but echo. But as he continued, the quality of the echo changed. It deepened. The space between his voice and its return grew longer, as though the room were expanding around him in a direction he could not see.

The flames on the braziers leaned inward. All of them, simultaneously, toward the center where he stood. Not flickering. Leaning. The way living things lean toward something that has caught their attention.

He kept speaking. The words were building toward something. He could feel it in the way the air thickened, in the way the stone beneath his feet grew slightly warm, in the way the inscriptions on the urns began to catch the firelight and hold it a fraction of a second longer than glass or stone should.

When he spoke the final syllable, the room answered.

---

Something spoke back.

A woman's voice. At first he thought it was [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Sylara Dawnmere|his mother]]. The timbre of it, the cadence, something in the shape of the sound reached directly into his earliest memories and pulled. But it was not her. It was something that knew exactly how to sound like her, something that had found the most unguarded place in him and pressed itself against it like a key against a lock.

The warmth was missing. That was the tell, arriving slightly after the recognition and slightly before the understanding. His mother was warmth the way the sun is warmth, not as one quality among others but as the foundational thing everything else was organized around. This voice had the exact acoustic shape of [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Sylara Dawnmere|Sylara Dawnmere]] and it was cold inside that shape. Not hostile. Not threatening. Simply absent of the thing that made the sound feel like safety.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/The Visage|It]] spoke to him.

It spoke his mother's name. [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Sylara Dawnmere|Sylara]]. Not with reverence, not with the careful distance of something recounting what it has learned from records or ruins. With familiarity. The way you say the name of someone you have known rather than someone you have heard of. It spoke the [[Campaign Knowledge/Organizations/The Dawnmere Lineage|Dawnmere]] name the same way, as something it had carried for a long time rather than something it had studied.

It named something he had never heard before. Dreamrot. The word arrived without explanation, placed into the space between them the way you place a stone on a table and wait. He did not recognize it. But something in the way it was spoken suggested it was not new to his story. Only new to his knowing of it.

It said it knew his father. Not the scholar. Not the man the universities remembered. It said it knew what [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had carried and what carrying it had cost him. It said it knew [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Sylara Dawnmere|his mother's]] secrets, the things she had held alongside her faith, the things she had chosen not to pass on. It did not say what any of these things were. It said it could. It said it would. For a price.

Then, as though reaching toward what he carried, it spoke of the pendant around his neck. It said the amulet held more than a family crest and a dead woman's faith. That what slept in the metal was older than his knowing of it and was waiting.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Sylara's Amulet|The amulet]] grew warm against his chest.

Not suddenly. It built. A gradual deepening of heat that started at the center of the pendant and spread outward through the metal like something waking by degrees. He pressed his hand against it without thinking and the warmth pushed back, steady and deliberate. Not hot. Warm. Like a hand on a shoulder in a dark room.

It spoke of [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|the journal]] at his side. It said there was more in those pages than a scholar's notes and a broken man's scrawl. That what lived between the covers had been waiting, just as the pendant had been waiting, just as the room itself had been waiting.

Against his side, [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|the journal]] trembled.

He almost did not notice. The voice was inside the room in a way that left very little space for peripheral attention. But the tremor was there, a single shudder along the cover like something shifting in its sleep. Some part of him registered it without being able to process it, filed in the place where things wait to be understood.

Beneath everything the voice said, beneath the shape of his mother's sound, there was desperation. Not the desperation of weakness. The desperation of something that has been waiting for a very long time and is finally close to what it needs. It needed something from him specifically. Not from anyone who performed the ritual. From him. From someone carrying [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Sylara's Amulet|the crest]] and whatever else he represented to it that he did not yet understand.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/The Visage|It]] asked him to become its servant.

The word was specific. Not ally, not follower, not partner. Servant. And beneath the asking there was an unspoken promise that the knowledge would follow, everything about his mother and everything his father had hidden, every answer to every question he had carried across the distance from a cold house to this room. All of it, on the other side of a single word.

He opened his mouth.

And something in him, something deeper than thought and older than reason, rooted in the place where his mother's warmth still existed despite everything, would not let him speak. Not as a word. As a feeling. The feeling that if he said yes in this room to this voice he would lose something he did not yet have a name for. That the knowledge being offered was real and the cost of accepting it was also real and he was not yet equipped to understand the shape of either one.

He ran.

No answer, no refusal, simply ran. The room erupted behind him. Skeletons wrenching free of crypts, braziers flaring white, urns shattering against stone. The floor heaved beneath his feet the entire length of [[Campaign Knowledge/Locations/The Sealed Ruin Beneath Grainfall|the ruin]], and he did not slow once, did not look back, ran straight through the entrance and into the street outside and grabbed the first person he found and told them that a god had awoken in the ruins beneath [[Campaign Knowledge/Locations/Grainfall]].

When the authorities went to look, [[Campaign Knowledge/Locations/The Sealed Ruin Beneath Grainfall|the ruin]] was exactly as it had always been. Cobwebs intact across every surface. Urns unbroken. Skeletons resting peacefully in their crypts. No sign of disturbance. No sign of anyone having been inside at all.

He was arrested for trespassing in a protected site and, in the eyes of everyone who heard his account, for being dangerously and publicly out of his mind.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Sylara's Amulet|The amulet]] was still warm against his chest when they led him away. It had not stopped since the voice began speaking.
