# The Descent

Then something changed in [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]].

It was slow. So slow that [[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] spent years explaining it away. His father was aging. His father was tired. His father had always been eccentric and perhaps eccentricity simply deepened with time. But the changes kept coming. [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] grew reclusive, then bitter, then quietly angry in ways that had no target and made no sense. He pushed [[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] out of his research. Then out of his home. Then, gently but unmistakably, out of his life. The warmth that had replaced [[Sylara Dawnmere|Sylara's]], imperfect and distracted and irreplaceable, began to go cold.

[[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had made what he called his ultimate discovery in [[Grainfall]]. He spoke of it rarely and cryptically. He returned to it obsessively. And he made absolutely certain his son never came near it.

Before his reclusiveness became total, [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had one last significant conversation with [[Brenvaal]], who had remained loosely in contact over the years. The two met and spoke at length, and [[Brenvaal]], who knew enough about what [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had found in [[Grainfall]] to form an opinion, argued against going further. He had seen something in what [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] was describing, or perhaps in the way [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] was describing it, that concerned him deeply. [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] listened. He considered the argument seriously, as he always did when [[Brenvaal]] spoke, because [[Brenvaal]] was not a man who alarmed easily. Then he made his decision. It had to be done. Whatever was in [[Grainfall]], whatever he had found there, he believed it was necessary to go back. They did not part badly. They parted as two people who respected each other enough to disagree without breaking. [[Brenvaal]] left. [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] returned to his work. That was the last time they spoke.

[[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] told himself the change in his father was jealousy, then dismissed that. It was unlike his father entirely. He told himself it was madness, then felt guilty for the thought. The truth that he could not yet name was simpler and worse. His father had found something in [[Grainfall]] that had found him back. And whatever it was, it had spent years slowly replacing [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon Ashdelve]] with something that wore his face and remembered his memories but had lost the thread of who he actually was.

In his final years, through what must have been extraordinary effort, [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] did the last loving thing he could manage. He pushed his son away. Not out of bitterness. Not out of madness. Out of the last remaining piece of himself that was still recognizably him. The part that knew, even through the fog of whatever was consuming him, that [[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] was more important than the work.

He died without explanation. Without reconciliation. Without ever telling his son what he had found.

He left only [[Aeralon's Journal|the journal]].

---

[[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] spent long nights alone in a cold and silent house, working backwards through his father's dense and meticulous notes. He had imagined this moment his whole life. The inheritance, the magic, the revelation his father had always promised. What he found was [[Aeralon's Journal|a journal]]. Extraordinary in its scholarship, precise in its observation, but a journal nonetheless. No hidden compartments. No encoded ciphers. Just his father's handwriting, page after page, growing gradually more erratic toward the end in ways [[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] did not want to look at too closely.

The final pages were nearly illegible. Dense and frantic, the handwriting of a man losing his grip on the instrument in his hand. Diagrams overlapping notes overlapping what appeared to be attempts at the same symbol drawn over and over, some careful and some clawed into the page. [[The Dawnmere Lineage|The Dawnmere crest]] appeared repeatedly in these pages, drawn obsessively in different sizes and orientations, every single one defaced. Crossed out, etched through, lines torn into the page with what must have been fury or fear or something that had stopped being one and become the other.

If this was the magic, why had it not protected him? If this was the inheritance, what had it given [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] that was worth keeping secret for a lifetime? [[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] turned to the first page.

His mother's handwriting.

[[Sylara Dawnmere]], dead twenty years, looked up at him from the page. She had written to [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] in the quiet, certain voice [[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] only half remembered. The one that made you feel held without being touched.

> _"We are taught by the ancients. We may dig to discover their truths and learn their secrets. But the purpose is to teach the next generation from their failures and successes. Don't spend your life searching for answers from them while becoming them now. You don't need to leave a message to the next generation. You are with them now. Keep searching. But remember to look up."_

She told his father who he was. Who he was supposed to be. That the ancients were teachers, not destinations. That the purpose of understanding the past was to be present for what was living in front of you. That he did not need to leave something behind for the next generation. He was with them now. Keep searching, she told him. But remember to look up.

And then, in handwriting that was slightly less steady, added at the bottom in what must have been her final days:

> _"Don't just look up. Point and show Zeph the joys the sky brings."_

Everything made sense. His father closing [[Aeralon's Journal|the journal]] on every dig and turning to show him something wonderful. Every museum, every ruin, every candlelit archive visited side by side. [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had not been indulging a curious child. He had been following instructions from the woman he loved, written in the one place he would always return to. [[Aeralon's Journal|The journal]] had not failed his father. His father had stopped opening it.

[[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] looked up.

Through a small dormer in the ceiling, the sunrise was happening. Pink and gold and entirely indifferent to grief, the way sunrises are. His mother's warmth finding him in a cold dark house, arriving exactly when he needed it, twenty years late and perfectly on time.

He reached for the wooden box on his father's desk. Inside, alongside the private things [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had kept close, was [[Sylara's Amulet|Sylara's amulet]]. [[The Dawnmere Lineage|The Dawnmere crest]] on its face caught the morning light in a way that seemed slightly too deliberate. He put it around his neck without fully knowing why. It felt like something that should travel with him.

That was when he decided.

Not out of tragedy. Not out of rage. Out of love, and inheritance, and the specific courage of someone who has been shown the sky by someone who loved them and cannot bear to stop looking.

He would go to [[Grainfall]]. He would find what his father found. He would understand what changed [[Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon Ashdelve]] in his final years, what killed him, and why he had spent those years doing everything in his power to keep his son away from it.

There was one more reason, quieter than the others, that [[Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] had not yet said aloud to anyone.

He had begun to notice things. Small things. Changes in himself. Subtle enough to dismiss, familiar enough to recognize. He knew this pattern. He had watched it for years in someone else's face.

He was his mother's son. And his mother's son does not look away from what needs protecting, even when what needs protecting is himself.

He packed his satchel, tucked [[Aeralon's Journal|the journal]] inside, and flew toward [[Grainfall]] with the sunrise at his back.
