# The Descent

Then something changed in [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]].

It was slow. So slow that [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] spent years explaining it away. His father was aging. His father was tired. His father had always been eccentric and perhaps eccentricity simply deepened with time. But the changes kept coming. [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] grew reclusive, then bitter, then quietly angry in ways that had no target and made no sense. He pushed [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] out of his research. Then out of his home. Then, gently but unmistakably, out of his life. The warmth that had replaced [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Sylara Dawnmere|Sylara's]], imperfect and distracted and irreplaceable, began to go cold.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had made what he called his ultimate discovery in [[Campaign Knowledge/Locations/Grainfall]]. He spoke of it rarely and cryptically. He returned to it obsessively. And he made absolutely certain his son never came near it.

Before his reclusiveness became total, [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had one last significant conversation with [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Brenvaal]], who had remained loosely in contact over the years. The two met and spoke at length, and Brenvaal, who knew enough about what Aeralon had found in [[Campaign Knowledge/Locations/Grainfall]] to form an opinion, argued against going further. He had seen something in what Aeralon was describing, or perhaps in the way Aeralon was describing it, that concerned him deeply. Aeralon listened. He considered the argument seriously, as he always did when Brenvaal spoke, because Brenvaal was not a man who alarmed easily. Then he made his decision. It had to be done. Whatever was in Grainfall, whatever he had found there, he believed it was necessary to go back. They did not part badly. They parted as two people who respected each other enough to disagree without breaking. Brenvaal left. Aeralon returned to his work. That was the last time they spoke.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] told himself the change in his father was jealousy, then dismissed that. It was unlike his father entirely. He told himself it was madness, then felt guilty for the thought. The truth that he could not yet name was simpler and worse. His father had found something in [[Campaign Knowledge/Locations/Grainfall]] that had found him back. And whatever it was, it had spent years slowly replacing [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon Ashdelve]] with something that wore his face and remembered his memories but had lost the thread of who he actually was.

In his final years, through what must have been extraordinary effort, [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] did the last loving thing he could manage. He pushed his son away. Not out of bitterness. Not out of madness. Out of the last remaining piece of himself that was still recognizably him. The part that knew, even through the fog of whatever was consuming him, that [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] was more important than the work.

He died without explanation. Without reconciliation. Without ever telling his son what he had found.

He left only [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|the journal]].

---

[[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] spent long nights alone in a cold and silent house, working backwards through his father's dense and meticulous notes. He had imagined this moment his whole life. The inheritance, the magic, the revelation his father had always promised. What he found was [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|a journal]]. Extraordinary in its scholarship, precise in its observation, but a journal nonetheless. No hidden compartments. No encoded ciphers. Just his father's handwriting, page after page, growing gradually more erratic toward the end in ways [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] did not want to look at too closely.

The final pages were nearly illegible. Dense and frantic, the handwriting of a man losing his grip on the instrument in his hand. Diagrams overlapping notes overlapping what appeared to be attempts at the same symbol drawn over and over, some careful and some clawed into the page. A crest, rendered in dozens of variations across the final coherent pages, every single one defaced. Crossed out, etched through, lines torn into the page with what must have been fury or fear or something that had stopped being one and become the other.

The symbol nagged at him. Something about the shape of it reached into a part of memory older than language, the part where early childhood stores things as feeling rather than image. He had seen this before. Not in any of his father's excavations. Not in any archive or museum. Somewhere closer and older than scholarship.

He reached for the wooden box on his father's desk without fully knowing why. Inside, alongside the private things [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had kept close, was [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Sylara's Amulet|Sylara's amulet]]. He turned it over in his hands. The crest on its face caught the faint light, and the match was immediate.

His mother's family crest. The [[Campaign Knowledge/Organizations/The Dawnmere Lineage|Dawnmere]] mark, which [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Sylara Dawnmere|she]] had worn her entire life as a cleric of holy light. The same symbol his father had drawn obsessively in the final pages of his life's work and destroyed every time.

He put it around his neck without understanding why [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had been fixated on [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Sylara Dawnmere|Sylara's]] crest, or why the fixation had taken the shape of destruction rather than study. The amulet felt like something that should travel with him. He left it on.

If this was the magic, why had it not protected him? If this was the inheritance, what had it given [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] that was worth keeping secret for a lifetime? [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] turned to the first page.

His mother's handwriting.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Sylara Dawnmere]], dead twenty years, looked up at him from the page. She had written to [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] in the quiet, certain voice [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] only half remembered. The one that made you feel held without being touched.

> _"We are taught by the ancients. We may dig to discover their truths and learn their secrets. But the purpose is to teach the next generation from their failures and successes. Don't spend your life searching for answers from them while becoming them now. You don't need to leave a message to the next generation. You are with them now. Keep searching. But remember to look up."_

She told his father who he was. Who he was supposed to be. That the ancients were teachers, not destinations. That the purpose of understanding the past was to be present for what was living in front of you. That he did not need to leave something behind for the next generation. He was with them now. Keep searching, she told him. But remember to look up.

And then, in handwriting that was slightly less steady, added at the bottom in what must have been her final days:

> _"Don't just look up. Point and show Zeph the joys the sky brings."_

Everything made sense. His father closing [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|the journal]] on every dig and turning to show him something wonderful. Every museum, every ruin, every candlelit archive visited side by side. [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon]] had not been indulging a curious child. He had been following instructions from the woman he loved, written in the one place he would always return to. The journal had not failed his father. His father had stopped opening it.

[[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] looked up.

Through a small dormer in the ceiling, the sunrise was happening. Pink and gold and entirely indifferent to grief, the way sunrises are. His mother's warmth finding him in a cold dark house, arriving exactly when he needed it, twenty years late and perfectly on time.

That was when he decided.

Not out of tragedy. Not out of rage. Out of love, and inheritance, and the specific courage of someone who has been shown the sky by someone who loved them and cannot bear to stop looking.

He would go to [[Campaign Knowledge/Locations/Grainfall]]. He would find what his father found. He would understand what changed [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Aeralon Ashdelve|Aeralon Ashdelve]] in his final years, what killed him, and why he had spent those years doing everything in his power to keep his son away from it.

There was one more reason, quieter than the others, that [[Campaign Knowledge/Characters/Zephyrix Skydelver|Zeph]] had not yet said aloud to anyone.

He had begun to notice things. Small things. Changes in himself. Subtle enough to dismiss, familiar enough to recognize. He knew this pattern. He had watched it for years in someone else's face.

He was his mother's son. And his mother's son does not look away from what needs protecting, even when what needs protecting is himself.

He packed his satchel, tucked [[Campaign Knowledge/Items/Aeralon's Journal|the journal]] inside, and flew toward [[Campaign Knowledge/Locations/Grainfall]] with the sunrise at his back.
